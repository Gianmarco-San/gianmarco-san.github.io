How the Monte Carlo mode works — and how to pick a sensible σ
What “Simple” does. One deterministic path. Each year the portfolio grows by exactly the expected return rate r (e.g. 7 %), and savings are added. Inflation is applied separately to costs & salary. This shows the average outcome — but in the real world returns vary, and the average outcome is not the typical outcome.

What “Monte Carlo” does. Each year, instead of using r exactly, a random shock is drawn from a normal distribution N(r, σ²) using the Box-Muller transform (two uniform samples → one Gaussian sample). Returns are clamped to [-50 %, +60 %] to avoid pathological tails. We run 500 / 1 000 / 2 000 independent paths through your full timeline (years on the x-axis, portfolio value on the y-axis), then sort the paths year-by-year to compute the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles. The shaded band is the 80 % confidence interval; the dark line is the median. Per-path FIRE years are aggregated into a “success rate” (% of paths that ever cross the FIRE threshold) and a median FIRE year.

How to pick σ. σ is the annual standard deviation of returns. Use historical reference points:
  • 5–8 % — bond-heavy / conservative portfolio, or a global multi-asset fund with low equity tilt.
  • 10–13 % — diversified 60/40 portfolio (S&P 500 + treasuries, post-1950).
  • 15–18 % — diversified all-equity portfolio (MSCI World, S&P 500 long-term).
  • 20–25 % — single-country emerging-market or sector-tilted equity (small-cap, tech-heavy).
  • 30 %+ — concentrated single-stock or crypto-style exposure. Tail-risk dominates; results are unstable.
Default σ = 15 % matches the long-run S&P 500 / MSCI World volatility. Lower σ ⇒ tighter band but the median path is essentially the simple projection. Higher σ ⇒ much wider band, more downside scenarios, and the success rate becomes very sensitive to your savings rate.

Caveats. The model assumes (a) returns are i.i.d. Gaussian, (b) σ is constant, (c) no fat tails, (d) no return-of-capital risk (e.g. a single-stock collapse). Real markets are mildly fat-tailed (kurtosis > 3) and exhibit volatility clustering (GARCH effects) — so the model slightly underestimates tail risk. For most retirement-planning purposes the bias is small relative to the σ-choice itself. The simulation is also memoryless: a bad year has no influence on the next.

      Training Plan Builder

      v1.0 · Apr 2026 — weekly plan + strength · 3 philosophies · 100% client-side
      🎯 Pace calibration — current best + goal time → Daniels VDOT pace zones in the plan
      Enter your current race time to compute VDOT and pace zones.
      Daniels VDOT (Jack Daniels, 1979 — refined 2014) maps a recent race time + distance to a VO₂max equivalent, then derives five training paces: E (easy, ~70 % effort), M (marathon, ~80 %), T (threshold / tempo, ~88 %), I (interval / VO₂max, ~100 %), R (repetition / speed, ~110 %). Math: VDOT computed via Daniels-Gilbert (already used by the VO₂max Estimator); paces solved from the inverse Daniels-Gilbert at fixed effort fractions. If you also enter a goal time, both VDOT levels and their pace zones are shown side by side so you can see the gap to close.
      The three philosophies
      • Polarized (80/20) — ~80% of weekly volume very easy, ~20% hard (intervals). Very little moderate "grey-zone" work. Based on Stephen Seiler's research on elite endurance athletes; emphasises high aerobic volume + a small dose of high-intensity stimulus.
      • Daniels (threshold-heavy) — Jack Daniels' VDOT system. Pace zones (Easy · Marathon · Threshold · Interval · Repetition) are derived from a recent race. Lactate-threshold (T) running is the cornerstone, training sustainable race pace efficiently.
      • Lydiard (base-build) — Arthur Lydiard's classic structure. A long aerobic base phase first (heavy easy mileage), then hills, then anaerobic intervals only as the race approaches. Volume drives improvement; intensity comes late.
      ⚠ General guidance — not medical, nutritional, or coaching advice. Listen to your body, take recovery weeks when fatigued, and consult a professional for injury, illness, or significant performance goals. Hydration / nutrition tips are generic. No data leaves your browser.

      Training Session Builder

      v1.0 · May 2026 — intervals · fartlek · progressive · tempo · long · TCX export for Garmin
      Saved sessions (this browser only)
        Heart-rate zones reference
        Zone%HRmax%HRR / %LTHREffort & purpose
        Z150–60 %50–60 %Recovery / very easy. Conversational. Fat oxidation, capillary density, recovery between hard days.
        Z260–70 %60–75 %Aerobic / easy. Still able to talk in full sentences. Bulk of weekly volume — mitochondrial, capillary, stroke-volume adaptations.
        Z370–80 %75–87 %Marathon / steady "grey zone". Phrases, not full sentences. Builds aerobic ceiling but high-cost; polarized models avoid it.
        Z480–90 %87–95 %Threshold / hard. Few words at a time. Sustainable ~30–60 min. Lactate-clearance and tempo work live here.
        Z590–100 %95–100 %VO₂max / max effort. Cannot speak. 3–5 min reps; develops top-end aerobic power and neuromuscular drive.
        HRmax ≈ 220 − age (rough). HRR (Karvonen) = HRmax − HRrest. LTHR (lactate-threshold HR) is more accurate than %HRmax if known. Best practice: tag each segment with the intended zone, then verify against your watch after the run.
        How averages are computed. For each segment we know distance d (km) and pace p (s/km), or time t (s) — whichever is supplied — and derive the missing one as t = d·p.
        · Active total: sum of d and t over rows tagged active; average pace = Σtactive / Σdactive.
        · Session total: sum across all rows (active + recovery); average pace = Σtall / Σdall.
        For a progressive run, drop all rows into "active" and the average pace falls out as the distance-weighted mean of the splits.

        TCX export. Generates a Garmin Connect-compatible Training Center XML file with one Course / Workout block. Open Garmin Connect → "Training" → "Workouts" → "Import workout" and pick the file; from there it syncs to your watch. Apple Watch is not supported (no public import format); a third-party app like HealthFit or RunGap would be needed to convert TCX → HealthKit.

        Saved sessions live only in this browser's localStorage (no server). Clearing site data wipes them.
